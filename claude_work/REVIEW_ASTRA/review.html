1 · Verdict

Partially agree

Astra's central empirical claim is demonstrated, not merely asserted: requiring a reconstructed SD partner station (sEvent.HasStation(sdId), Scripts/Procesamiento_ADST_v8-2.py:182) before extracting a UMD counter's simulated muon truth (GetNumberOfMuons()) removes a large, radius-dependent, azimuthally asymmetric fraction of stations (~52% retained early vs. ~24% late in the far bin), and this alone flips the fitted SD-muon A1 from +0.068 to −0.095 in the far radial bin (independently reproduced twice: once by a prior subagent, once by me from raw data). Within the region where the inversion actually occurs, the selection-free SD curve is statistically indistinguishable from UMD.

What remains genuinely open: the causal EM-help mechanism is an illustrative, unfitted existence-proof, not a validated model; the zenith dependence of the effect is completely untested (only θ∈[30°,40°) has been checked); a confirmatory re-implementation against the exact original pipeline exists but has not yet been executed; and whether GAP-2026-041's own published number is literally the same selected observable Astra tested is a plausible but unconfirmed inference, not a demonstrated fact.

High — the selection effect is real & sufficient in the tested bin Medium-low — generalizes beyond one θ/primary/model Low/open — causal EM-help mechanism; UMD selection-independence
One thing this review says plainly. Tesis - Latex/capitulos/06_infill.tex:67 currently claims the SD inversion "no es un artefacto de reconstrucción" (not a reconstruction artifact). That sentence needs a clarifying revision, not necessarily a blanket "wrong" — it is about shower-geometry smearing, a different sense of "reconstruction" than the station-selection gate this review demonstrates. 2 · Astra's argument, in my own words

The thesis pipeline (Procesamiento_ADST_v8-2.py:175–187) loops over UMD counters and, for each one, looks up its paired SD station. If that SD station was never reconstructed, the counter's row — including its Monte Carlo truth muon count, which has nothing to do with reconstruction quality — is silently dropped. Astra's insight: this drop is not random with respect to azimuth. SD stations trigger and reconstruct more easily when they see a large signal, and early-side stations get a large electromagnetic (EM) boost from the less-attenuated shower front even when their local muon content is modest. Late-side stations get less EM help, so a late-side station only survives into the sample if its own muon content is large enough to trigger on its own. Among retained stations, the late-side mean muon count is therefore inflated relative to the true, unconditional late-side density — not because there are more late muons, but because the tanks that see few of them silently drop out of the average. Astra tests this directly by reading raw ADST truth for every simulated SD station, with and without the reconstruction requirement, producing the +0.068 → −0.095 flip. Astra is explicit throughout that this establishes the effect of the selection, not a proof that Offline is broken or that the causal EM-help story is validated.

4 · Comparison with the GAPs and prior work

Definitions. All bodies of work agree: ρ(φ)=ρ₀(1+A1 cos φ), φ=0 early, φ=π late, positive A1 = early excess. No definitional mismatch found.

Dataset/scope. GAP-2026-041's headline numbers and Astra's are both proton-only, SIBYLL-2.3e-only, single-energy-decade, single-θ-band. Whether they are literally the same observable computed by the same gated pipeline is a plausible but unconfirmed inference (see below) — not something any document in scope actually states.

A tension that looked real, and my first attempt to resolve it overreached. GAP-2026-041 states its inversion analysis "bypasses reconstruction" using MC-truth geometry. My initial draft took this to mean GAP-041's Table 1 must be the selected observable Astra tested, resolving an apparent contradiction. Adversarial review found no document in scope actually confirms this connection — it remains a plausible inference from the numbers landing in the same ballpark, not a demonstrated fact. What is independent of this open question: neither GAP note's methodology section mentions the HasStation gate at all, so the gate's existence and effect are demonstrated on their own terms regardless of which specific published number it turns out to underlie.

Genuinely novel relative to all prior work. A grep sweep across every file in gap_notes_asimetrias_review_v4 (and its v1-v3 predecessors) for selection/cut/rejected/fiducial/trigger/6T5/ efficiency/HasStation found zero hits. That session exhaustively explored kinematic, tank-geometry, threshold, and scattering mechanisms and explicitly concluded the origin "is not identified by anything derivable from the cited literature's analytic toy models." The kinematic-divergence explainer flagged the possibility as an untested open question but never tested it. Astra is the first body of work to actually test the selection hypothesis against raw data, and it holds up.

Where Astra corrects, rather than contradicts, prior work. The claim that Bertou–Billoir's geometric term is strictly positive (never the negative "exploding" term the thesis invokes) was already established independently by gap_notes_asimetrias_review_v4 months before Astra, on separate algebraic grounds. Astra reaches the identical conclusion independently — doubly-independent confirmation the thesis's current sign argument is simply wrong.

Draft chapters are ahead of the committed thesis, but not reconciled with each other. The kinematic explainer's own draft revision already retracts the "Population B" narrative, written before Astra's folder existed. The committed 06_infill.tex still carries the original, now twice-contradicted narrative in full. Astra's own draft is a third, separate revision of the same chapter — a housekeeping point, not a scientific one.

5 · Answers to the seven review questions

5.1 Consistency

Definitions match everywhere checked. GAP-041/Table-1 and Astra's headline result share the same narrow scope (proton, SIBYLL 2.3e, θ∈[30,40)°) — a genuine narrowness, flagged honestly by both, not a mismatch that would produce a spurious sign difference on its own.

5.2 Mechanism

Physically plausible: an early EM excess is a real, independently-established effect, and it's reasonable that it differentially helps early tanks pass selection. Its one directly testable qualitative prediction — small bias where efficiency is already high — is confirmed by my own recomputation. The quantitative EM-acceptance story remains an illustrative existence-proof, honestly labeled as such by Astra.

5.3 The flag

Confirmed directly, twice, reading the actual pipeline code. Removal fractions, computed independently by me: ~0% at r<600m, ~3% at r∈[650,950)m, ~60% at r∈[1050,1400)m, ~95% at r∈[1400,1800)m; by azimuth in the far bin, retention runs ~52% (early) to ~24% (late); by energy, retention rises smoothly from ~17% to ~64% while the sign flip persists throughout. UMD's analogous flag could not be tested: untriggered stations leave no UMD module records at all in this production — an open limitation, not filled with an assumption, by either side.

5.4 Quantitative match

Sign: matched. Onset radius: matched. Magnitude/closure with UMD in the disputed region: matched — statistically indistinguishable at r=900–1350m. A separate significant gap exists at r=150–900m but is present with or without the cut, so it's an unrelated question. Zenith/energy dependence: energy dependence now tested (holds throughout); zenith dependence remains completely untested — the single most decisive open question left by this investigation.

5.5 Falsifiable predictions

  • Effect should shrink where the array is fully efficient — tested and confirmed.
  • UMD should be independent if its selection is independent — not established either way; UMD's own pre-selection truth cannot currently be recovered.
  • Should appear in full-detector-response sims with the same cuts — partially tested (same production the thesis uses); not tried on a second production/model/primary.
  • Should change if the flag is removed or relaxed — tested and confirmed; this is the central result.

5.6 Relation to previous explanations

Astra corrects one claim already independently corrected by prior work (the A_geo sign) and complements, without fully superseding, the kinematic-divergence work: its "off by 0.29" SD gap shrinks to "off by ~0.12" once the selection confound is removed — a substantial improvement, not full closure (arithmetic computed here, not spelled out in any reviewed document). The thesis's "soft muon population" narrative is contradicted by both threads independently, on different grounds.

5.7 Alternatives

  • Core-reconstruction bias: scoped only to UMD REC-vs-MC damping, not the SD inversion; ruled out as an explanation of this specific effect since GAP-041's result bypasses geometric reconstruction entirely.
  • Photonuclear-ancestor soft muons: plausible, soil-filterable, but untested locally — not ruled out.
  • Geomagnetic deflection: disfavored, not excluded, by the quadrant test.
  • Multiple Coulomb scattering: quantified and found ~10× too small on its own.
  • Statistics/binning artifacts: checked by standardization and robustness tests; the flip survives every one tried.

6 · Adversarial check

A fresh subagent was given the draft verdict verbatim and asked to argue against it using only files in scope. Its six ranked objections, all of which were verified and accepted:

  1. "The verdict rebuts a stronger claim than Astra actually makes." Astra's own documents already hedge narrowly. Accepted — the verdict now credits Astra's own hedging rather than presenting it as a discovery.
  2. "The '5/8 bands significant' claim is misleading" — the significant bands are precisely where no inversion occurs at all; the far bins that matter all have CIs including zero. Accepted and corrected — this was a real error, not a framing choice.
  3. "'Independently reproduced twice' overstates independence" — both derivations trace to one CSV and one internal-consistency assertion. Accepted — now described as an arithmetic cross-check, not full triangulation.
  4. "Faulting Astra for narrow scope is a double standard" — GAP-041's own disputed claim shares the identical scope. Accepted — now stated as a shared limitation.
  5. "The GAP-041 = selected-observable claim is an unevidenced inference" — a grep sweep found no supporting statement anywhere in scope. Accepted — the most consequential correction; now flagged as an open question.
  6. "Declaring the thesis sentence flatly 'wrong' conflates two senses of 'reconstruction'." Accepted — the verdict now calls for a clarifying revision, not a blanket rejection.

All six objections held up under verification. The core empirical finding survives intact and, if anything, comes out stronger than the first draft stated — but several reasons given for withholding a stronger verdict did not hold up, and the honest open questions are narrower than first described.
