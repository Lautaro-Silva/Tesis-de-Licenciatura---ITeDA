Second correction notice — read this first

The first version of this report missed required prior reading; the second version corrected that but proposed a "muon-attenuation sign correction" sourced from Bertou & Billoir's own muon-density-vs-depth measurement as a real, partial (~20%) contribution toward the SD's missing physics.

That candidate is now retracted, not just re-quantified. B&B's measurement comes from a paper about the SD, using an essentially unthresheld muon population — not the UMD's hard ~1.22 GeV threshold — and applying it to the UMD side mixed in a population the UMD never sees. Worse: B&B's own text ("total decreases, but spread increases") describes lateral spreading, which is very plausibly the same physics the kinematic term (§2) already models — properly — meaning the correction likely double-counted, with the wrong sign, from the less reliable of two sources. §3 documents this as a retracted dead end, in the spirit of CLAUDE.md §6's Fast-MC record, not deleted from view.

§1The unified model: (i)–(iii) are one bracket

Armbruster (KIT 2018; GAP-2020-066; Luce et al.'s Ref. [6]) derives, expanding the exact conical-apex geometry to first order in $\varepsilon=r/d(\theta)$:

\[ S(r,\psi) = S(r)\,(1+a\cos\psi), \qquad a = \left(2-\gamma+\frac{d(\theta)}{\lambda}\right)\frac{r}{d(\theta)}\tan\theta \]

The "+2" is the geometric $1/d^2$ solid-angle projection, "$-\gamma$" the kinematic/ADF term, "$+d/\lambda$" attenuation — three additive terms in one bracket. Checked against exact geometry at the reference point ($r=1200$ m, $\theta=35°$, $D=7.5$ km):

WCD tank particle count+0.034+0.032
WCD tank muon VEM—0.000 (Cauchy theorem, exact)
Double-counting to avoid

Bertou & Billoir's $A_{geo}=\langle p_r/-p_z\rangle\tan\theta\approx(r/d)\tan\theta$ is numerically identical to the linearised "+1" aperture term (both $=0.112$). Listing $A_{geo}$ and a separate "flat-plane aperture" term as two additive instrumental biases double-counts.

§3A retracted lead: the muon-attenuation-sign candidate

3.1  The candidate, as originally proposed

Neither GAP note, nor the explainer notebook, checks whether muon attenuation is early-favoring like the EM component's. Bertou & Billoir's own MC (GAP-2000-017, Fig. 1, particles at 900–1200 g/cm² of vertical depth, for $10^{19}$–$10^{20}$ eV vertical showers) reports, verbatim: "the muonic density is increasing, at least up to 1200 g.cm⁻² (of course, the total number of muons decreases, but their spread increases)… the contribution of muons reduces, and possibly reverses, the asymmetry." This looked, at first, like a real, correctly-signed candidate.

3.2  Why it's retracted — two compounding problems, found by direct questioning

Problem 1 — population/threshold mismatch (decisive)

B&B's Fig. 1 is raw AIRES particle output — essentially the entire muon spectrum down to the simulation's own low-energy cutoff, the same population the SD tank is sensitive to. The UMD only sees muons above 1.22 GeV. §2 already shows the kinematic term is highly threshold-sensitive. B&B's curve, dominated by soft muons, almost certainly measures a population the UMD's soil shielding removes entirely. Applying the same correction factor to both detectors — as the previous version of this report did — mixes a population the UMD doesn't sample into the UMD's own number.

Problem 2 — likely double-counting with the kinematic term already in the model

B&B's own description — a declining total, spreading to a larger radius with depth — is a description of lateral spread, exactly what Cazón's kinematic-divergence term (already in §2's combination, spectrum-weighted properly) is a model of. If these are two measurements of the same phenomenon, B&B's factor on top double-counts it, with the opposite sign, from the cruder of the two sources.

A third, secondary problem from the first round of correction: B&B's calibration points (900–1200 g/cm²) sit above the reference point's own slant depths (568–708 g/cm²) — any use requires both extrapolation and an unjustified energy transfer across two orders of magnitude, without correcting for the elongation-rate shift in $X_{max}$.

None of these is fatal on its own to using B&B's finding for the SD alone, with the population and depth-range issues resolved. Together, applied the way the previous version applied them, they are fatal to the specific "$+0.14$" / "~20% of the gap" numbers reported.

What this retraction establishes

No candidate mechanism found across this session, or any of the five prior passes, survives scrutiny as an independent, correctly-attributed contribution to the SD gap — not even partially. A stronger, cleaner negative result than the previous version reached, and it makes the case for cutting the current thesis derivation more solid, not less: no remaining partial analytic account exists, only the sharp, specific, already-known open question about the SD's own observable definition (§2).

§4Cazón's own $Q$ uncertainty

cazon2012.pdf never states a numerical $Q$; the commonly-used $Q=0.2$ GeV isn't supportable from this citation — Cazón's Fig. 8 median implies $Q\approx0.12$–0.13 GeV, a factor-1.6 discrepancy. Cazón's own point is that a single $Q$ or $D$ is inadequate. This affects every number in §2, including the explainer notebook's own.

§5New this pass: a real-MC acceptance audit, run and killed

Direct pipeline audit found the A1 fit is an unweighted mean per $\phi$ bin over however many real 750 m-array stations project into that bin — no exposure normalisation exists anywhere in the pipeline; quality flags are computed but never used as cuts.

  • Table 2 reproduces well: $A_1^{UMD}(r{=}1200)=+0.068$, $A_1^{SDmu}=-0.093$, vs published $+0.11/-0.10$.
  • The raw station count per $\phi$ bin carries a large, $r$-growing modulation: $-0.02$ at 450 m to $-0.32$ at 1200 m to $-0.58$ at 1600 m — a pure acceptance artifact.
  • Across 42 $(\theta,r)$ cells, strongly anti-correlated with $A_1^{SDmu}$ ($r=-0.89$, $p=1.6\times10^{-15}$), uncorrelated with $A_1^{UMD}$ ($r=-0.08$).
The direct causal test kills this

Equalising per-bin sample count via bootstrap resampling and re-fitting $A_1$ leaves both observables essentially unchanged at every radius — shifts of $+0.0002$ to $+0.0018$, all inside the bootstrap's own spread. The correlation is real; it is not causal. A genuine negative result: closes off a candidate fifth mechanism no prior pass tested, because none worked with the real parquet.

§7Overall verdict and recommendation for the thesis text

Direct answer to the director's binary question

Cut the current derivation. This session did not find a way to avoid that conclusion — it found two candidates (a decisive one from prior work, a speculative one of its own) and neither survives.

Cut this

The derivation as currently written in 06_infill.tex / GAP-2026-041 (Population B / kinematic-filter attribution, and the volumetric/track-length mechanism) should not go into the thesis. Now the conclusion of five independent passes: the mechanism doesn't just fail to reach the right magnitude, it predicts the detector ordering backwards.

The UMD side is essentially already explained

By prior work this session verifies and credits: kinematic divergence, properly spectrum-weighted, plus the flat-plane $A_{geo}$ term reproduce the measured $+0.11$ to within $0.02$. No unidentified physics needed there.

The SD side remains genuinely open

This session tried one new candidate and retracted it, for reasons documented in §3 rather than silently dropped — that retraction is itself useful: it rules out (for now) a plausible-looking shortcut and leaves the one sharp, specific, actionable open question from prior work (§6 item 3) as the most promising next step, not a fifth analytic toy model.

Recommended reduced claim for the thesis

Present the SD inversion and UMD stability as a robust, real, MC result, shown not to be a pipeline artifact (§5). State that the UMD's asymmetry is well-reproduced by kinematics + flat-plane geometry — no unidentified physics needed. State plainly that the SD's inversion is not reproduced by the same framework, that no analytic mechanism checked across six passes of this investigation (including a specifically-tried muon-attenuation-sign correction, documented as a dead end) closes that gap, and that the sharpest remaining lead is a code-level question about the SD's own observable definition, not another analytic model.

What NOT to do

Do not claim the muon-attenuation-sign idea (§3) explains any part of the SD inversion, even partially. Two rounds of trying to quantify it — first "comparable to the gap," then "~20% of the gap" — both turned out to rest on errors a moment's more scrutiny caught. The lesson: check whether a borrowed number depends on the source detector's own construction (mass, threshold) before reusing it across detectors, rather than keep re-quantifying a candidate that hasn't earned it.
