01

The question that prompted this

“Given the geometry fixed you determine what angles α late and early you need, but you are not weighing that angle sample by the probability of sampling it given the energy you are working on. That means that if you need a big angle and you are working with high energy you should be penalised.”

The objection is half right, and the half that is right invalidates a published-in-the-notebook result. Section 2 shows what the model already did correctly; Section 3 shows the two places where it did not.

02

What the per-energy calculation already did right

The angular distribution is evaluated at each of the two required angles separately, at the energy under consideration. So the “penalty for needing a big angle at high energy” is present — it is the factor exp(−E sinα/cQ) computed at αearly and at αlate. The subtlety is that the penalty applies to both regions, and hits the early one (larger angle) harder. That asymmetric penalty is the mechanism.

The spatial (dearly/dlate)² factor is 0.645 at this point, i.e. an early-favouring handicap of 1/0.645 = 1.55. The table says the angular term only overcomes it somewhere between 2 and 8 GeV — which is exactly the crossover E* ≈ 2.5 GeV found independently in Section 3 of the notebook. Low-energy muons are the population least able to generate a late excess, not the most.

03

Two errors in the earlier spectrum-weighted calculation

Error 1 — an average of ratios, not a ratio of averages

The earlier version computed

$$\left\langle \rho \right\rangle=\frac{\int E^{-\gamma}\,\rho(E)\,dE}{\int E^{-\gamma}\,dE},\qquad \rho(E)=\frac{S_{late}(E)}{S_{early}(E)}$$

weighting every energy by the raw production spectrum. Its own docstring admitted the assumption: “assumes the early-region density at each E is simply proportional to N(E)”. It is not — the early region demands the larger angle and is therefore exponentially harder to populate at every energy. Weighting both regions identically discards precisely the selection effect being measured. The correct object weights each energy by how many muons of that energy actually arrive in that region:

$$\frac{S_{late}}{S_{early}}=\frac{d_{early}^{2}}{d_{late}^{2}}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\int N(E)\,f(\alpha_{late}\mid E)\,dE}{\displaystyle\int N(E)\,f(\alpha_{early}\mid E)\,dE}$$

Error 2 — the ADF’s own energy-dependent normalisation

Deriving the angular distribution properly from Cazón’s transverse-momentum law rather than quoting its shape:

$$P(p_t)=\frac{p_t}{Q^{2}}e^{-p_t/Q},\qquad \sin\alpha=\frac{cp_t}{E}\;\Longrightarrow\; f(\alpha\mid E)=\frac{k^{2}}{2\pi}\,\frac{\cos\alpha\,e^{-k\sin\alpha}}{1-e^{-k}(1+k)},\qquad k\equiv\frac{E}{cQ}$$

The k² prefactor cancels in the late/early ratio at fixed E — which is why dropping it never affected the crossover energy — but it does not cancel under a spectrum integral, because it changes which energies dominate. Physically: an energetic muon squeezes its emission into a solid angle ~1/k², so per muon it delivers k² times more density in whatever direction it does go. Including it turns the effective weight from E−γ into roughly E2−γ.

Consequence — the divergence was never physical

The old calculation ran away to A1 → −1 once Emax passed ~100 GeV, and that was explained away as “the toy model breaking outside its calibration”. It was simpler than that: it was the wrong integral. Done correctly, A1 = +0.160 at Emax = 20, 100, 2000 and 20 000 GeV alike — high-energy muons are exponentially suppressed in both regions, so they can never dominate.

04

The corrected result, and what it says about low energy

At the reference point with γ = 2.6, the corrected spectrum-weighted kinematic term gives A1 = +0.160 — early-favouring, and dominated by the 1/d² dilution. The low-energy population does dominate the arriving flux, exactly as the steep spectrum implies, but it contributes almost no asymmetry of its own because (Fig. 1) its angular distribution cannot resolve the 2° gap. Breadth washes the asymmetry out; it does not create it.

05

The decisive test: vary only the threshold

With the calculation fixed, the model makes a prediction that can be checked against the two detectors directly — and it fails in a specific, informative way.

The headline result

Changing nothing but the threshold, the model predicts the UMD should be less early-favouring than the SD, by about −0.14. The simulations report the opposite: UMD = +0.11 against SD-Muon(MC) = −0.10, i.e. UMD more positive by ~0.21.

This is not “the mechanism is too small”. As formulated, it orders the two detectors backwards. The narrative in which the UMD’s overburden acts as a kinematic filter — removing the population responsible for the late excess and thereby preserving a positive attenuation asymmetry — does not survive this check: within this model, raising the threshold enriches the late-favouring population rather than removing it.

06

Folding in each detector’s own geometric response

The kinematic term is not the whole prediction: each instrument adds its own geometric response, already derived earlier in the notebook — a flat buried plane for the UMD (A1 = +0.109, uncancelled because its observable is a raw count) and the WCD tank’s projected aperture for the SD (A1 = +0.031, the top/side trade-off).

The constructive half of the result

Attenuation aside, the UMD is reproduced essentially correctly by kinematic divergence plus flat-plane projection alone (+0.13 vs. +0.11 measured). The framework fails only on the SD, and fails by ~0.29 in amplitude. That is a far more useful statement than “no mechanism works”: it says the unexplained physics is specific to the surface detector, not to the muon flux as such.

07

Radial behaviour

Note also that the model’s UMD-like curve turns slightly negative beyond ~1500 m, whereas the measured UMD amplitude stays positive and flat. So even the successful half of Section 6 should not be over-read: it works at the reference radius, not necessarily across the whole range.

08

Consequences for the thesis text

Chapter 3 (draft). The paragraph that previously deferred the threshold question to Chapter 6 has been rewritten, since the question is now answerable and the answer is negative. It now states the filter expectation, evaluates it, reports that A1 falls from +0.16 to +0.02 across the SD→UMD threshold change and crosses zero above ~2 GeV, and concludes that kinematic divergence is a real and correctly derived mechanism which nevertheless does not by itself explain the measured detector contrast.

Chapter 5 (draft). Unaffected. Its A1–vs–energy mechanism is the 1/D geometric term, checked separately against the Xmax elongation rate (right sign, ~40–65% of the measured magnitude).

Chapter 6 (not yet revised). Still carries the Population B narrative for the SD inversion, which is now doubly unsupported: wrong population (crossover energy), and wrong detector ordering (this memo). This is the largest remaining inconsistency in the thesis.

10

Caveats, and what would settle it

What this is. A closed-form toy model: a single production point at fixed D, a single pt scale Q, ballistic propagation, no attenuation term, and no geomagnetic deflection. It is useful for one thing — checking whether a stated equation behaves the way surrounding prose claims — and it has now done that for several claims.

What it is not. A shower simulation. In particular the absolute predicted values inherit the choice D = 7.5 km and γ = 2.6, and the single-production-point assumption is known to matter (§3b). The relative statement — that raising the threshold moves A1 downward — is far more robust than any absolute number, since it holds across every (D, γ) combination tried.

Questions worth putting to the advisor

1. Does the group accept that the UMD-as-kinematic-filter argument should be dropped from the GAP note and the thesis, given it predicts the detector ordering backwards?

2. Is the SD’s MC-truth muon count at the tank boundary (GetNumberOfMuons()) genuinely a count of incident muons, or does it already carry a detector-level selection that the toy model cannot represent? The framework reproduces the UMD and fails only on the SD, which makes the SD-side observable definition the first thing to re-examine.

3. Is per-muon production data (energy, height, angle) retrievable from the existing icrc2025-test7 production, or would settling this need a dedicated CORSIKA run?
