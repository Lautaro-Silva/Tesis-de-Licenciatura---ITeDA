## The core question: was cutting the kinematic-divergence argument justified?

**Independent assessment: yes — and the strongest reasons are internal to the derivation and its own cited literature, not the detector-geometry confound raised in the first pass.**

In descending order of force (full derivation of each in `kinematic_divergence_math_check.md`):

**1. The energy attribution in Version_Vieja's Eq. (9) is inverted.** The late-favoring exponential gain, `exp((sin α_early − sin α_late)E/cQ)`, is *monotonically increasing* in E — it is small near $E=0$ and only matters once $E$ is large. Evaluated with exact geometry and parameters sourced from the note's own cited references ($Q\approx0.2$ GeV from Cazón 2012, $D\approx5$–10 km from Cazón and from Bertou & Billoir's own "~5 km altitude" statement), the crossover energy $E^*$ where the net kinematic term flips from early- to late-favoring sits at **1.9–3.7 GeV** at the disputed reference point ($r=1200$ m, $\theta=35°$, $D=7.5$ km) — at or above the UMD's own effective threshold of $E_\mu\gtrsim1$ GeV$/\cos\theta\approx1.2$ GeV. Population B (defined by the note itself as $E\lesssim1$ GeV) sits almost entirely in the *early*-favoring regime, the same sign as attenuation — the opposite of what the note claims. The narrative isn't falsified for all production heights ($D\lesssim2$–3 km would bring $E^*$ below threshold), but it's unevaluated at the $D$ the note's own citations point to.

**2. It silently duplicates already-published, refereed work — and gets the sign wrong relative to it.** Luce et al. (ICRC 2021) §2.2 already model the geometrical effect via $\Delta\Omega\propto1/d^2$ and an angular distribution function $\mathrm{ADF}(\delta)\propto(\delta/\delta_0)^{-\gamma}$, deriving `α ∝ 2 − γ + d(θ)/λ` — term-for-term Version_Vieja's Eq. (9), with Version_Vieja's exponential ADF corresponding to a *local* slope $\gamma_{eff}=E\alpha/cQ$ instead of a constant $\gamma$. Reproducing $2-\gamma_{eff}$ numerically gives the same $E^*\approx2.5$ GeV. Version_Vieja cites `Luce_ICRC2021` only as the source of the *observation* of the SD inversion, never engaging with its own quantitative model. Worse: Luce's simulated data (their §2.2/Fig. 3) states the muon-component amplitude is "almost null ($\le0.05$)" out to 2 km — never approaching the $-0.10$ both GAP notes report for the same quantity (SD-Muon(MC) at $r=1200$ m, Table 2). The note cites Luce as supporting evidence for an inversion twice the size Luce's own simulation shows.

**3. The tank-volumetric MC-truth confound** (the first pass's lead argument, now demoted to third): the MC-truth "muon" count used in the §4.3 decomposition, $N^{MC}_{\mu,\mathrm{sup}}$, is the particle count incident on the 3D volumetric boundary of the WCD tank, not an idealized flat ground plane — and Bertou & Billoir (2000) already showed this specific instrumental effect (§6–7) can suppress a muon early/late asymmetry. GAP-2026-041 §4.3 later adds the sentence acknowledging this that Version_Vieja never states.

**4. Eq. (9) omits attenuation entirely** and is nonetheless compared directly against the observed (attenuation-inclusive) $A_1$ in §4.3 — circular, since the thing to be explained (the observed inversion) is used as confirmation that the un-attenuated exponential gain dominates.

**Verdict: cutting it was the right call, but for a more specific and more damaging reason than "needed more review."** The derivation assigns the effect to the wrong energy population, and does so while silently re-deriving (and contradicting the magnitude of) a result already published by the same collaboration's own cited reference.

## But the published fix does not repair the physics — it relocates one problem and introduces another

**GAP-2026-041 did not remove the kinematic hypothesis — it demoted it.** The derivation (§2.2, Eqs. 4–9) was cut, but the same low-energy attribution survives almost verbatim in §6 (Conclusions) ¶2, now hedged with "plausibly": *"low-energy muons … are emitted with wide divergence angles … The projection of its broad emission cone … plausibly generates a massive statistical particle-density excess in the … late region."* Moving a claim from the framework section to the discussion section lowers its epistemic weight in the note's structure, but the statement itself is the same one shown wrong in sign above (point 1). A reader of only §6 would still be misled.

**The replacement instrumental mechanism (§6 ¶3) is quantitatively wrong in sign — this is the sharpest finding of this pass.** GAP-2026-041 attributes to Bertou & Billoir a track-length/side-wall effect that "contributes negatively to the overall harmonic asymmetry." Read in full, B&B's own conclusion for muons is *compensation*, explicitly: "we expect the 'early' muonic signal to be the same as the 'late' one," and their own MC check (Fig. 6, $10^{20}$ eV, $\theta=30°$) shows the total signal staying **positive** ($A_1\approx+0.11$ to $+0.13$) out to 2.4 km, never crossing zero; their §7 caps the total asymmetry at "less than a factor of 1.4 at $40°$." Nothing in the cited source predicts an inversion. Checking the track-length claim directly (this pass, not in either source): for a parallel beam through a convex body, the Cauchy mean-chord relation makes the total path length exactly $F_\perp\cdot V$, *independent of incidence angle* — the WCD's muon VEM response is angle-independent, **exactly zero geometric asymmetry**, not negative. At the reference point ($r=1200$ m, $\theta=35°$): flat plane (UMD-like) $A_1=+0.109$; WCD particle count $A_1=+0.031$; WCD muon VEM $A_1=0.000$ (exact). The tank suppresses the geometric term toward zero, it does not reverse it. **Both of GAP-2026-041's offered mechanisms for the SD inversion point the wrong way when checked against the note's own cited sources.**

This does not mean the SD inversion in the MC is spurious — it's the note's own simulation output and there's no reason to doubt the number itself — but its physical explanation, as given in both GAP note versions, is not established. This is the same category of error the supervisor's original instinct correctly flagged in Version_Vieja; the published fix inherited it rather than resolving it.

## The largest open gap: the UMD's own instrumental asymmetries (new this pass)

The note's central claim is that the UMD's positive, monotonic $A_1$ traces atmospheric attenuation cleanly, because the UMD is "essentially planar" with "no vertical exposure." But a flat, buried detector has two purely instrumental, purely positive asymmetry sources that neither GAP note subtracts:

1. **Flat-plane geometric projection ($A_{geo}$ itself).** Both notes correctly state, quoting Bertou & Billoir, that $A_{geo}=\langle p_r/-p_z\rangle\tan\theta$ is *strictly positive* (§2.1.2) — and then never apply it to the UMD, whose horizontal scintillator plane retains this term in full (it's exactly what the "flat plane" row above computes): $A_1\approx+0.11$ at the reference point, the same order as the entire measured UMD signal.
2. **Azimuthally modulated overburden threshold.** Both notes write the UMD threshold as $E_\mu\gtrsim1\,\mathrm{GeV}/\cos\theta$ and note "its selectivity itself depends on zenith angle" — but the angle that matters is the *local* incidence angle at the detector, which is larger in the late region than the early region for the same reason the tank's side-wall exposure is (both are consequences of the same geometric projection). At the reference point, $\theta_{loc}\approx24.8°$ (early) vs $43.2°$ (late), giving $E_{thr}\approx1.10$ vs $1.37$ GeV — a purely instrumental positive $A_1$ of **+0.11 to +0.22** depending on the assumed muon spectral index, comparable to or larger than the measured UMD $A_1\approx+0.11$.

Taken together, these are order-of-magnitude, single-production-point estimates, not corrections to be plugged in — but they are large enough that the claim "the UMD's $A_1$ is attenuation-driven" is not established by either GAP note. If they turned out to dominate, the intrinsic (flux-level) muon asymmetry could in principle be smaller, or even of different sign, for both detectors than either note's narrative assumes.

## Tension with the note's own cited sources on the size of the inversion

- Luce Fig. 3/§2.2: muon-component amplitude "almost null ($\le0.05$)." GAP-2026-041 Table 2: SD-Muon(MC) $=-0.10$ at 1200 m.
- B&B Fig. 6: total WCD signal stays positive, $A_1\approx+0.11$–$0.13$, out to 2.4 km. GAP-2026-041 Table 2: SD Total (VEM) $=-0.08$ at 1200 m.

Different energies/arrays ($10^{17.75}$ eV / SD-750 vs. $\sim10^{19}$–$10^{20}$ eV / SD-1500 in the cited sources), so not strict contradictions — but the lower-energy shower analyzed in the GAP notes is *older* at ground and should show *more* positive attenuation asymmetry, not less. The note reports a larger inversion than either of its own cited sources for the inversion's existence, and reconciles neither discrepancy. This would be the first question a referee asks.

## Other differences between Version_Vieja and GAP2026_041 worth flagging

- **Title/framing shift**: "A Phenomenological Study of Early–Late Azimuthal Asymmetries... with the UMD" (confident, solved-problem framing) → "...UMD–SD Comparison and Insights into the Surface-Detector Sign Inversion" (investigative, open-question framing; §3.3 explicitly calls it "the central puzzle"). Appropriate given the weakened causal claims.
- **Iron cross-check dropped**: Version_Vieja's Table 1 lists "Proton, Iron (analysed independently)" and asserts iron shows "identical qualitative behaviour" — but no iron plot is ever shown in the note. GAP2026_041's Table 1 lists only "Proton." Removing an unshown claim was right, but it leaves the note's forward-looking mass-composition motivation with zero supporting evidence in-note (the thesis's own Ch. 5 Merit-Factor material could have carried this).
- **New instrumental-mechanism section**: GAP2026_041 §6 ¶3 adds a full derivation of the side-wall/track-length mechanism that has no counterpart in Version_Vieja. Genuinely new text — but, per above, wrong in sign when checked against its own cited source and against an exact geometric theorem.
- **Numbers unchanged.** Figures 2–5 and Table 2 are numerically identical between versions; only the causal interpretation layered on top changed. The measurement itself is stable.
- **Statistics, both versions.** "Unweighted least-squares fits" throughout, with no per-point weighting, no reported χ²/ndf or goodness-of-fit, uncertainties collapsed to a single "typically $\le0.02$," and no systematic-uncertainty budget at all — no hadronic-model spread (only Sibyll 2.3e is used, despite EPOS-LHC-R and QGSJET-III.01 being available in the same production per CLAUDE.md §9), no thinning-level check ($10^{-6}$, quite aggressive), and no accounting for the Dense Ring's 5×-resampled cores breaking the independence an unweighted fit assumes.

## Overall opinion on the GAP notes generally

The published note (GAP2026_041) is better-hedged in its title and framing than Version_Vieja, and its central *empirical* result — UMD stays positive and monotonic, SD inverts at large $r$ — is a real, stable measurement across both versions. But its causal story for *why* is not an improvement on Version_Vieja's; it is the same underlying error (over-attributing an observed effect to a specific, checkable mechanism without checking it against the cited literature) relocated from the kinematic argument to the volumetric one. A referee at EPJC level would raise, in order of severity: (1) neither offered mechanism for the SD inversion is supported in the direction claimed, by the note's own cited sources; (2) the reported inversion magnitude exceeds what either cited source's own numbers would predict, unreconciled; (3) the UMD's own instrumental asymmetries (flat-plane $A_{geo}$, modulated threshold) are never subtracted, so the headline "UMD is attenuation-clean" claim is asserted, not demonstrated; (4) no systematic-uncertainty budget of any kind. None of these are fatal to the underlying physics program — the UMD vs. SD contrast is a genuinely interesting, novel measurement — but the note as it stands does not establish its own explanatory claims, only the raw contrast.

The thesis, once finished, should not simply adopt GAP-2026-041's two-mechanism framing as authoritative; it should either run the discriminating checks proposed below itself, or explicitly flag in Ch. 6 that the physical origin of the SD inversion (and the cleanliness of the UMD result) remains genuinely open.

## Consequence for the thesis text specifically

`Tesis - Latex/capitulos/03_fenomenologia.tex:88` states $A_{geo}$ "posee signo opuesto necesariamente" (has necessarily the opposite sign, relative to attenuation); `:94` says the term "explota a grandes distancias con signo negativo" (explodes at large distances with negative sign) for Population B; `:101` says the UMD's filter makes it "colapsa matemáticamente a cero." `06_infill.tex:84,94,119,154` build the entire SD-inversion narrative on $\langle p_r/-p_z\rangle$ going negative. With the sign convention both GAP notes use ($p_r>0$, $-p_z>0$), this ratio is strictly positive and *grows* with divergence — making $A_{geo}$ *more* positive, not negative. This is a sign error in the thesis's own algebra, not an interpretive difference from the GAP notes: both GAP notes' §2.1.2 already state $A_{geo}$ is "strictly positive" correctly. The thesis did not inherit this fix, and the deferred Toy-Model roadmap at `06_infill.tex:119` is premised on demonstrating something algebraically false as currently framed. This needs correcting at the level of the equation, not softened wording — and, per the discussion above, even the corrected framing (kinematic divergence, or tank/track-length) is not yet established as the SD inversion's actual cause, so Ch. 6 should be written to reflect that the mechanism is open, not settled.
