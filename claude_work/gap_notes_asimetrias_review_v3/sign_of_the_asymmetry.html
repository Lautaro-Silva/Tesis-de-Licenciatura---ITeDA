01

Was cutting Version_Vieja's kinematic section justified?

Yes — and the strongest reasons aren't the one raised first.

Version_Vieja's §2.2 explained the SD's far‑core sign inversion with a kinematic‑divergence term: low‑energy “Population B” muons, it argued, are emitted at wide angles and pile up in the late region faster than they thin out with distance. The director asked whether that gain term is actually large where the paper needs it to be — at low E. Checking it directly says no, and for a more specific reason than “not clearly demonstrated.”

The ratio being fit

Eq. (9) of Version_Vieja writes the late/early ground‑muon density ratio at fixed shower‑plane radius r as three multiplied factors:

$$\frac{S_{late}}{S_{early}} \approx \underbrace{\left(\frac{d_{early}}{d_{late}}\right)^{2}}_{\text{spatial, }<1} \cdot \underbrace{\left(\frac{\cos\alpha_{late}}{\cos\alpha_{early}}\right)}_{\text{phase-space, }>1} \cdot \underbrace{\exp\!\left(\frac{(\sin\alpha_{early}-\sin\alpha_{late})\,E}{cQ}\right)}_{\text{exponential, }>1\text{, grows with }E}$$

The algebra checks out — re-deriving dN/dΩ from dN/dpt ∝ pt e−pt/Q reproduces the paper's own Eq. (8) exactly. But look at the third factor's own derivative:

$$\frac{d}{dE}\exp\!\left(\frac{(\sin\alpha_{early}-\sin\alpha_{late})E}{cQ}\right) = \frac{\sin\alpha_{early}-\sin\alpha_{late}}{cQ}\cdot(\cdots) \;>\;0$$

Strictly positive whenever αearly > αlate — which is always, by construction. The late‑favouring gain is monotonically increasing in energy. It is small near E = 0 and only matters once E is large. Population B is defined by the note itself as the low‑energy population. The term the paper leans on doesn't intrinsically favor the population it's pinned to — it favors the opposite end of the spectrum.

At the reference point with citation‑sourced parameters (Q ≈ 0.2 GeV from Cazón 2012's own Figs. 7–9; D ≈ 5–10 km from Cazón's worked example and Bertou & Billoir's “~5 km altitude” statement), the crossover sits at or above the UMD's own threshold for every production height the cited literature actually points to. Population B's own energy range is mostly on the early‑favouring side of that line — the same sign as attenuation, not the opposing one the paper's prose claims.

Independent confirmation — Luce et al., ICRC 2021 (#435)

Both GAP notes cite this proceedings paper only for the SD inversion's existence. Read in full, its §2.2 already publishes the same mechanism, with the sign question resolved:

$$\alpha \;\propto\; 2 - \gamma + \frac{d(\theta)}{\lambda}$$
“at large distances, the value of the exponent γ of the ADF can be large enough to compensate the attenuation and produce a negative amplitude of the asymmetry.”Luce, Roth, Schmidt & Veberič — ICRC 2021, §2.2

Term for term, this is Version_Vieja's Eq. (9): +2 is the squared‑distance factor, −γ is the angular gain, +d/λ is attenuation. The exponential ADF is just a special case with a local slope γeff = Eα/(cQ) instead of a constant γ — which is exactly what exposes the energy dependence above. Version_Vieja re-derives this from scratch without engaging it as prior art. And Luce's own simulated muon‑component amplitude is reported as “almost null (≤ 0.05)” — never approaching the −0.10 both GAP notes report for the same quantity at the same radius.

Why the paper missed this

Version_Vieja never computes E*. It asserts the gains “easily overcome” the spatial loss because collisions “overwhelmingly produce particles…at small emission angles,” then treats the observed SD inversion itself as confirmation that the gain dominates — the thing to be explained, used as evidence the explanation is right.

02

The published fix didn't repair the physics

It relocated one problem, then introduced a second of the same kind.

GAP‑2026‑041 didn't remove the kinematic hypothesis — it demoted it. The derivation (§2.2) is gone, but the same low‑energy attribution survives almost verbatim in the Conclusions, now hedged with “plausibly”:

“low-energy muons…are emitted with wide divergence angles…The projection of its broad emission cone…plausibly generates a massive statistical particle-density excess in the…late region.”GAP-2026-041, §6 (Conclusions), ¶2

Moving a claim from framework to discussion lowers its billing, not its correctness — and it's the same statement shown wrong in §1.

The replacement mechanism, checked

The note's new argument (§6 ¶3) attributes to Bertou & Billoir a tank track‑length effect that “contributes negatively to the overall harmonic asymmetry.” Read in full, B&B's own conclusion for muons is compensation, not inversion:

Bertou & Billoir · GAP-2000-017, §6
“the signal of a muon is roughly proportional to the length of water crossed…This compensates the surface factor, and we expect the ‘early’ muonic signal to be the same as the ‘late’ one.”
Same, §7 — their own MC check
“the total signal asymmetry is found to be less than a factor of 1.4 at 40°” — i.e. |A1| ≲ 0.17, never inverting, in their Fig. 6 out to 2.4 km.

Nothing in the cited source predicts a sign flip. And the track‑length claim doesn't survive a direct check either: for a parallel beam through a convex body, the Cauchy mean‑chord relation makes total path length exactly F⊥·V — independent of incidence angle. The 1/cosθloc track‑length “gain” the note invokes exactly cancels the cosθloc aperture loss it's layered on top of. For the muon VEM response, the geometric asymmetry is exactly zero, not negative.

Net result

Both mechanisms GAP‑2026‑041 offers for the SD inversion — the inherited kinematic attribution and the new volumetric one — point the wrong way once checked against the note's own cited sources. This doesn't mean the inversion in the MC is spurious; it's the note's own simulation output. It means the note's explanation for it isn't established.

03

What actually explains the SD

A completed account, not just a list of ruled-out mechanisms — added after review.

The author's own analysis confirms the SD/UMD split is physical — reproduced with Dense Ring and Infill topologies, MC and REC geometry — and pushed back that §§1–2 above never say what actually drives it. Fair. Re-deriving the tank effect more carefully, and testing a hypothesis this uncovered, gets somewhere.

The tank, done exactly

The Cauchy cancellation in §2 isn't just true for an idealized uniform flux — it holds region by region, for whatever the true flux is doing in that region, because aperture and mean chord are reciprocal at every angle individually:

$$\text{count} = F_\perp \cdot A_\perp(\theta_{loc}) \qquad \text{mean chord} = \frac{V}{A_\perp(\theta_{loc})} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \text{total path length} = F_\perp \cdot V$$

So the muon VEM signal is a clean, undistorted tracer of the true incident flux ratio — the tank's geometry contributes zero net bias to it, always, not just on average. That lets the true-flux ratio be backed out of what is reported: the raw MC-truth count, which only carries the (uncancelled) aperture term.

Because the aperture bias is early‑favouring, it's partially masking the inversion in the raw count — the true flux has to be more inverted than −0.10 to produce what's reported. Falsifiable prediction: an isolated muon‑only VEM signal (not currently broken out in either GAP note) should show a larger inversion than the muon count, not a smaller one — directly testable in the existing MC, no new simulation needed.

Does kinematic divergence survive being done properly? No.

The obvious next question: does the true flux's late-favouring behaviour come from kinematic divergence after all, once correctly weighted over the real muon spectrum instead of evaluated at one convenient energy? Tested directly — and this retracts a hypothesis raised in conversation, that a rare high‑energy tail above E* might dominate the average.

The true physical origin of the SD's inferred flux-level inversion is not identified by anything derivable from the cited literature's analytic models. That's a stronger, more useful statement than either GAP note makes — both assert a mechanism; nothing checked here survives.

Why the UMD looks cleaner — a hypothesis, not a finding

Since the true mechanism isn't identified, “the UMD filters it out” can't currently be claimed — there's no confirmed it to filter. What's defensible independently: the UMD carries two of its own instrumental terms (next section), both robustly early‑favouring, both simple geometry/threshold facts unrelated to the unresolved question above. Unlike the tank's VEM channel, the UMD's headline observable is a raw count — no Cauchy cancellation available to it.

The hypothesis

The UMD isn't cleaner because it removes the cause of the SD's inversion — it's cleaner because its own construction happens to inject a positive bias large enough to swamp whatever the true, still-unidentified late-favouring effect is doing, while the SD's construction happens (by the Cauchy accident above) to expose that same effect with no comparable protection. Testable: strip the UMD's own instrumental terms out and see if its signal shrinks toward — or past — zero at large r. See §8.

04

The UMD's own blind spot

Neither version checks whether the UMD's clean result is clean.

The note's headline is that the UMD's positive, monotonic A1 traces atmospheric attenuation cleanly, because the UMD is “essentially planar” with “no vertical exposure.” But a flat, buried detector still has two purely instrumental, purely positive asymmetry sources — both already visible in equations both notes already write down, neither of them subtracted.

1 · Flat‑plane geometric projection

This is Bertou & Billoir's own Ageo, which both GAP notes correctly call strictly positive — and then never apply to the UMD, whose horizontal scintillator plane retains it in full.

2 · Modulated overburden threshold

Both notes write Eμ ≥ 1 GeV/cosθ and note it's angle‑dependent — but the relevant angle is local, and it's larger in the late region for the same reason the tank's side wall is exposed there.

At the reference point, these two terms are individually +0.11 (flat‑plane projection) and +0.11 to +0.22 (threshold modulation, depending on β) — comparable to or larger than the entire measured UMD amplitude of +0.11. These are order‑of‑magnitude, single‑production‑point estimates, not corrections to plug in. But they're large enough that “the UMD's A1 is attenuation‑driven” is asserted, not demonstrated, in either GAP note.

05

Tension with the note's own cited sources

Two comparisons a referee checks first — and the note doesn't run either one.

Independent of the sign question, the magnitudes GAP‑2026‑041 reports for the two components it says drive the inversion are larger than what its own cited sources report for the same quantities, at (roughly) the same regime. Neither discrepancy is reconciled in the note.

The lower‑energy shower analyzed in the GAP notes (1017.5–18.0 eV, SD‑750) is older at ground than Bertou & Billoir's 1020 eV / SD‑1500 reference, and should therefore show more positive attenuation asymmetry, not less — the direction of the gap is the wrong way round for a trivial energy‑scaling explanation. This would be the first question a referee asks.

07

Consequence for the thesis text

A sign error in the algebra, not a difference of interpretation.

Both GAP notes' §2.1.2 state Ageo is “strictly positive” — correctly, since with pr > 0 and −pz > 0 the ratio can't go negative. The thesis, in Spanish, says the opposite in four places, and builds the entire Ch. 6 SD‑inversion narrative on it:

03_fenomenologia.tex:88
“…posee signo opuesto necesariamente.”
Claims Ageo necessarily has the opposite sign to attenuation. It doesn't — both GAP notes fixed this. Needs correcting at the equation level.
03_fenomenologia.tex:94
“…explota a grandes distancias con signo negativo.”
Same term, same error, for Population B specifically.
03_fenomenologia.tex:101
“…colapsa matemáticamente a cero.”
Correct that the UMD suppresses the term — but the premise it's collapsing from (a negative value) is the same sign error.
06_infill.tex:84, 94, 119, 154
SD‑inversion narrative built on ⟨pr/−pz⟩ “exploding…con valores negativos.”
The deferred Toy‑Model roadmap at :119 is premised on demonstrating something algebraically false as currently framed.

This needs correcting at the level of the equation, not softened wording. And per §§1–3 above, even the corrected framing (kinematic divergence, or tank/track‑length) isn't established as the SD inversion's actual cause — nothing checked so far survives — so Ch. 6 should read as an open mechanism, not a settled one.
